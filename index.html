3D lidar: laser pulses → 3D points → a map in memory

Range error vs distance (measured against the simulator's true range)

Why lidar stays sharp far away

Lidar range comes from timing, r = c·Δt/2, so its error stays about the same at 3 m or 25 m. A stereo pair works from a tiny disparity, so its error grows with Z². The dashed curve shows what a 30 cm stereo rig would give.

Same last step as the other pages

Once a return is a 3D point in the sensor frame, putting it on the map is the same y = R(θ)·p + t, 3×3 with a height. Lidar skips the matching and depth-from-disparity steps of stereo, so p comes straight from range and angles.

Where lidar struggles

Only 16 rings, so floors show as widely spaced circles and thin things between rings can be missed. Glass, black or shiny surfaces and heavy rain return poorly. There is no colour, so memory is coloured by height instead.

Honest limit

The sensor pose (θ, t) comes from the simulator's ground truth, and each 360° sweep is treated as instantaneous. A real robot estimates its pose with SLAM or an IMU and must correct for motion during the spin.

Teaching model: 16 rings (−20° to +15°), 720 azimuth steps (0.5°), 30 m range, 2 cm range noise, 1% dropouts, sensor 1 m above the floor, one full sweep every 0.5 m of travel.
